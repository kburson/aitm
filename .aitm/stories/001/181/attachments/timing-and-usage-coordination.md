## Timing model to carry into design review

- **Store evidence once.** Preserve provider-neutral project, story/draft, attempt, phase, actor/role and logical turn identity. Retain timestamped workflow events and half-open UTC intervals for proven turn, in-turn tool, owner-request/response, and other wait or idle boundaries, with evidence basis and coverage. Keep event occurrence and recording times distinct. Carry a consistent as-of cutoff, relationship snapshot, and metric version into derived totals. Late proof or corrections produce a new view with provenance. Private provider handles and transcript contents stay local.
- **Elapsed delivery time answers how long it took.** Store the start and verified finish events used for the selected scope. Present one calendar duration between those boundaries, including waits and idle inside it. An open item may display age through its as-of cutoff. A missing boundary is Unknown. Name the start basis, such as first attributable story work for all-phase delivery or Develop entry for implementation delivery. An epic uses its own scoped start and finish. Child elapsed durations are never added to compute epic elapsed time.
- **Actual answers how much agent effort went into it.** Union qualifying intervals per logical actor to avoid double-counting turn and tool evidence, then sum distinct actors, including concurrent work. Tool time inside a qualified story turn counts. Owner wait, idle and unattended execution outside a turn are excluded. Roll up each descendant once and add direct epic planning and orchestration. Show decimal agent-hours with phase, actor/role, and coverage drilldown. Preserve a separate optional union of work intervals across agents to show how much wall time had observed work. It is distinct from delivery elapsed and additive effort.
- **Wait and cost remain independent.** An owner wait starts on an evidenced request and stops on response or resolution. One agent can wait while another works. Keep owner wait, other wait and idle distinct from the effort total, and avoid adding overlapping categories into a wall-clock total. Cost comes from uniquely attributed provider tokens and a rate basis. Tool runtime adds no charge by itself.
- **Display both measures from one snapshot.** Use the board's **Actual** for additive agent-hours and a separate elapsed delivery measure for calendar time. Put the boundary basis and coverage in the detail view and show partial status alongside any published subtotal. The elapsed field name and placement, and the canonical start/finish event choices for each report scope, belong in the #181 design review.

For a replay fixture, child A works 09:00–10:30, child B works 09:30–11:00, an owner response is outstanding from 11:00–11:30, and direct epic coordination runs 11:30–11:45. With kickoff at 09:00 and verified delivery at 11:45, the elapsed delivery time is **2h 45m**, the wall-clock union of observed work is **2h 15m**, and epic Actual is **3.25 agent-hours**. Any overlapping observations of one actor count once.

## Calibration rule

Compare a retained estimate with Actual for the **same phase window and agent scope**. Pin the estimate revision, baseline, applicable attempt or outcome, included roles, and timing basis. Keep an implementation estimate paired with implementation-window Actual for its declared roles and clock basis. An all-phase or inclusive epic figure is a different measure. Retain the estimate, observed subtotal, and scope or coverage mismatch for diagnosis. Only complete, comparable records enter calibration. Partial and Unknown records remain inspectable for lessons and measurement gaps.

The [backfill](2026-11-10-actual-backfill.md) illustrates the distinction. #111's estimate was 2.5 implementation agent-hours. The observed board total was 0:36:27 across pickup through closure. The narrower Develop-to-review-pass observation was 0:28:31 across participants. Both are partial historical observations. Neither is an eligible estimate-error sample.
